Key idea 1 — zero padding: the smooth curve is the same physical finite-observation spectrum whether the display uses N FFT points or 8N points. Zero padding places more sample points on that same curve. It can make the peak location look smoother, but it does not create new measured information.

Key idea 2 — why changing Tc may not change the range plot: at fixed bandwidth B, Δfb∝1/Tc and the finite-record spectral width is also proportional to 1/Tc. Therefore their ratio is unchanged. After converting the horizontal axis from beat frequency to range, the normalized range spectrum is essentially invariant to Tc. The beat-frequency geometry above makes the two changes visible before they cancel on the range axis.

Δfb = 2BΔR/(cTc),   Δfobs≈1/Tc,   Δfb/Δfobs=2BΔR/c,   ΔRnom≈c/(2B).

Try this: move only Tc. Watch both frequency quantities shrink together while the range spectrum stays nearly unchanged. Then move B and observe that the range spectrum really does change.
